Cand-01

EU/US Active-Active Dual-Region Lakehouse (full 99.99% topology)

Eliminated
Topology

BTS-Synthetic Enterprise Data Platform deployed as two fully independent, simultaneously live stamps: primary in Azure West Europe (Netherlands), secondary in East US 2, each stamp spanning three availability zones. Storage is ADLS Gen2 (ZRS within region) holding Delta/Iceberg tables; EU-resident tables are pinned to the West Europe stamp via per-table residency policy and are never replicated westbound, while non-EU and globally-shared reference data replicates bidirectionally through the platform's own log-shipping/Delta replication layer rather than raw GRS.

IoT ingest (40k devices, 80k events/sec peak) lands on Azure Event Hubs with a geo-aware IoT ingest endpoint fronted by Azure Front Door / Traffic Manager, with regional Event Hubs namespaces so EU device telemetry terminates in EU. Compute is the platform's premium multi-region active-active add-on: warmed query clusters in both regions, an active-active metastore/catalog with conflict resolution, and a shared control plane replicated across both. Networking is hub-and-spoke per region — Azure Virtual WAN hubs, Private Link/private endpoints for all storage and platform services, ExpressRoute from Austin and Munich R&D plus the three manufacturing sites, no public data-plane exposure.

Power BI serves 600 users via DirectQuery against the geographically nearest stamp with an EU-only gateway path for EU-resident datasets. Batch ETL from the 30+ internal sources (including the Teradata estate being decommissioned through 2027) runs on the EU stamp with the US stamp consuming replicated curated tables. Failover is DNS/Front Door-driven with no data-loss window for curated tables.

Optimised for

RFP 4.3 (99.99% monthly uptime — the only candidate that architecturally underwrites it, and the only one that survives the "any SLA failure of any duration terminates the contract" clause); 2.3 multi-region deployment read literally as both regions live; 2.3 EU data residency via per-table pinning rather than region-wide crudeness; 2.2 peak ingest headroom (250k events/sec tested vs 80k required, headroom surviving the loss of a whole region); 2.1 real-time IoT ingest with regional termination; single-vendor accountability for the uptime commitment.

Obvious weakness

Cost and complexity. Per Technical Fit this requires the bespoke active-active add-on at roughly $80K–120K/year on top of base platform and Azure infrastructure, and it doubles the Azure footprint (storage, compute, Event Hubs, ExpressRoute, egress for cross-region replication) — all quoted under a 5-year fixed price with no escalators (3.1) and a mandated 35% floor discount (3.3). Margin exposure is severe and one-way. It is also the highest delivery-risk build: an active-active catalog with EU residency carve-outs is a bespoke configuration, not a shipped default, and it must be stood up inside an aggressive timeline that also carries a Teradata migration.

Position paper — Candidate 1 advocate

1 · Why this wins on the RFP's actual requirements

RFP 4.3 is not a soft target — it demands 99.99% monthly uptime and states that any SLA failure of any duration triggers immediate termination plus full refund of that month's fees. That is a zero-tolerance clause with an uncapped-liability backstop (4.1) sitting right behind it. Per Technical Fit, the standard Enterprise tier is only 99.95% — architecturally incapable of carrying that risk. Candidate 1 is the only topology built to actually underwrite 99.99%: warmed query clusters live in both regions, an active-active metastore/catalog with conflict resolution, and DNS/Front Door-driven failover with no data-loss window for curated tables. We are not bidding a number we hope holds; we are bidding the only architecture that structurally supports the number the customer wrote into the contract.

RFP 2.3 asks for "multi-region deployment (primary EU, secondary US East)." Acme's own CDO, Marcus Webb, will read that phrase the way any technical buyer does — as two regions that are actually serving traffic, not a label slapped on a cold DR box. Candidate 1 is the only one of our three options where "secondary" means live, not dormant. That matters directly against evaluation criterion 1 (functional fit, 30% weight) — the highest-weighted line on the scorecard.

On residency (2.3, "EU customer data must remain in EU"), we don't rely on region-wide crudeness — we pin EU-resident tables per-table, and EU IoT telemetry terminates in EU via regional Event Hubs namespaces, never crossing the Atlantic even in transit. That is a defensible, auditable answer to RFP 4.2's unannounced, up-to-four-times-a-year audit right: we can show an auditor exactly which tables are EU-pinned and prove they've never left, rather than arguing about a shared-region posture.

On scale, RFP 2.2 requires 80,000 events/sec peak ingest. We test to 250K events/sec — and critically, that headroom holds even after losing an entire region, because both stamps carry independent capacity. Acme is asking us to absorb their Teradata decommissioning (through 2027) at the same time as steady-state growth of ~12TB/month; a topology that still has 3x headroom after a regional failure is the one that doesn't wobble under that combined load.

2 · Delivery risk case

The individual building blocks here are not exotic — ADLS Gen2 with ZRS, Event Hubs, Virtual WAN hub-and-spoke, Private Link, ExpressRoute — these are pieces our team stands up in every large Azure engagement, including the ExpressRoute circuits into Austin, Munich, and the three manufacturing sites that Acme already needs regardless of which topology we pick. The genuinely new work is scoped narrowly: the active-active metastore/conflict-resolution layer and the per-table residency-pinning policy engine. That is a bounded, well-understood integration surface, not a ground-up build — we are extending a proven platform capability, not inventing distributed-systems primitives from scratch.

Sequencing also works in our favor against the Teradata cutover: because both stamps run active-active from day one, we can cut EU batch ETL over first (where the bulk of Acme's 30+ internal sources and existing Teradata dependencies sit) while the US stamp comes up in parallel consuming replicated curated tables — a phased path that de-risks the highest-stakes migration work rather than making it a single-region choke point.

3 · Cost case

Yes, this is the most expensive of our options to run — that is the direct consequence of it being the only one that actually satisfies RFP 4.3. But look at the TCO shape Acme itself is scoring against: evaluation criterion 3 (TCO, 20%) sits behind criterion 1 (functional fit, 30%) and alongside criterion 4 (implementation risk, 15%). A cheaper architecture that cannot honestly carry the SLA clause isn't a lower-TCO option — it's an architecture with an uncosted tail risk of immediate termination plus full-month refund (4.3) stacked on top of uncapped breach liability (4.1). Price that contingency into any "cheaper" alternative and the comparison changes.

The shape of our bid: base platform + Azure infrastructure, doubled footprint for storage/compute/Event Hubs/ExpressRoute/cross-region egress, plus the active-active add-on (~$80–120K/year per Technical Fit) — all locked under the 5-year fixed-price, no-escalator term (3.1) and the 35% discount floor (3.3). That is real margin pressure, and we should say so plainly rather than have it discovered later. But it is pressure taken on once, at signature, in exchange for zero exposure to the recurring, uncapped downside of 4.3/4.1 for the life of a 5-year deal.

4 · Known weakness, addressed head-on

The honest risk here is cost and delivery complexity, not technical feasibility. This is the highest-cost bid of the field and it is a bespoke configuration — active-active catalog plus EU residency carve-outs is not a shipped, click-to-deploy default. We should not let pricing or Acme's procurement team discover this after the fact: we will state in the commercial proposal, plainly, that the add-on premium and doubled infrastructure spend are what a genuine 99.99% floor costs, and that the alternative — bidding a lower SLA tier or a single-region topology — reintroduces exactly the termination and uncapped-liability exposure that 4.3 and 4.1 create. On delivery, we mitigate by scoping the net-new engineering to the metastore/conflict-resolution and residency-policy layers only, reusing proven platform components everywhere else, and by sequencing EU-first to keep the Teradata runway from colliding with the dual-region buildout. We would rather present this trade-off ourselves, with a phased delivery plan attached, than have the commercial skeptic or the customer surface it as a surprise.

Cand-02

EU-Primary Zone-Redundant Lakehouse with Warm US East DR

Selected
Topology

A single active region — Azure West Europe — running the platform in its standard Enterprise tier but deployed zone-redundant across all three availability zones: ADLS Gen2 in ZRS, Event Hubs zone-redundant namespaces, platform compute pools spread across zones with zone-aware scheduling, and a zone-redundant metastore. All 280TB of primary data and all EU customer data live only in West Europe, which makes residency (2.3) a property of the deployment rather than a per-table policy that has to be enforced and audited.

East US 2 exists as a warm standby: infrastructure-as-code pre-provisioned landing zone, curated Delta tables asynchronously replicated on a schedule (RPO measured in tens of minutes), minimal idle compute, promoted to primary only on a declared regional failure. IoT ingest terminates on a single global Event Hubs front door in West Europe with device-side buffering at the edge gateway for transient outages; batch ETL and the Teradata decommissioning pipelines all run in West Europe.

Power BI DirectQuery from 600 users hits West Europe, with a curated import/aggregation layer plus a US-side read cache for Austin analysts to mask transatlantic latency on high-traffic dashboards. Networking is a single hub-and-spoke with Private Link, ExpressRoute into the EU hub, and the US spoke dormant but wired.

Optimised for

RFP 2.2 scale at the lowest sane cost (280TB and 12TB/month in one region, no duplicate storage bill); 2.3 EU residency in its simplest, most auditable form — useful against 4.2's unlimited no-notice audit right, since there is one region to show an auditor; 3.3/3.1 commercial pressure, since it leaves the most room to absorb a 35% discount on a 5-year fixed price; evaluation criterion 4 (implementation timeline and risk, 15%) — it is the standard shipped topology, so it is the fastest to stand up and the least likely to slip against the Teradata 2027 decommissioning runway; 2.1 workloads all met on the default product.

Obvious weakness

It cannot honestly promise 99.99%. Zone redundancy raises real-world availability but the contracted Enterprise tier is 99.95% (Technical Fit), and RFP 4.3 makes any SLA miss of any duration a termination-plus-full-month-refund event. This candidate either forces us to bid a lower SLA than the RFP demands — a scoreable gap against Databricks/Snowflake/Fabric who will bid to the number — or forces us to accept a 99.99% contract our architecture does not underwrite. It also reads as non-compliant with "multi-region deployment (primary EU, secondary US East)" if Acme interprets that phrase as two serving regions rather than DR, and it puts 600 users' interactive BI on a single-region blast radius with a US read experience that is second-class.

Position paper — Candidate 2 advocate

1 · Why this wins on the RFP's actual requirements

Strip away the aspirational language in RFP 2.3 ("multi-region deployment, primary EU, secondary US East") and look at what Acme is actually buying: a replacement for a Teradata patchwork (Section 1), at 280TB and 12TB/month growth (2.2), for 600 Power BI users and 150 engineers (2.1), under a fixed 5-year price with a mandatory 35% floor discount (3.3) and 30-day no-cause termination (3.5). Every one of those facts argues for the leanest architecture that satisfies the letter of the requirement, not the most expensive one.

RFP 2.3 says "primary EU, secondary US East" — that is a primary/secondary pair, not a mandate for two simultaneously-serving production regions. This candidate delivers exactly that: West Europe as the live primary, East US 2 as a warm, pre-provisioned, infrastructure-as-code standby that promotes on declared failure. It satisfies the clause as written without inventing a requirement Acme didn't ask for.

On residency, this candidate makes compliance structural rather than procedural: all 280TB and all EU customer data live in one region, full stop. That matters directly against 4.2 — Acme's unlimited, no-notice, vendor-pays audit right, up to four times a year. An auditor walking in unannounced needs to inspect one region's controls, not adjudicate a per-table pinning policy across two live stamps. That is a materially easier, cheaper, faster audit story, and audit cost is explicitly ours to bear under 4.2.

On scale, 2.2's 80,000 events/sec peak ingest and 280TB/12TB-month volumes are comfortably served by a single zone-redundant region — Technical Fit confirms streaming tested to 250K events/sec headroom exists in the base product before any multi-region premium is layered on. We are not paying to duplicate infrastructure to solve a scale problem Acme doesn't have.

Finally, on evaluation weighting (Section 5): Commercial terms (25%) and TCO (20%) together outweigh functional fit (30%) by only 5 points, and Implementation timeline and risk (15%) is a full category unto itself. This candidate is built to win on three of five criteria without giving up the fourth.

2 · Delivery risk case

Response is due 2026-05-26, award expected 2026-06-30 — a 14-day RFP window, and Acme is running the Teradata decommission through 2027 in parallel with whatever we stand up. This candidate is our standard Enterprise-tier shipped topology deployed zone-redundant across three AZs — a supported, repeatable pattern our field teams have stood up before, not a bespoke build. Zone-redundant ADLS Gen2, zone-redundant Event Hubs namespaces, zone-aware compute scheduling, and a zone-redundant metastore are all first-class, documented deployment options, not custom engineering. The East US 2 side is infrastructure-as-code and dormant — provisioned once, validated, and left warm, which is a fraction of the effort of standing up and continuously operating a second live production stamp. That timeline profile is exactly what a 15%-weighted "implementation risk" criterion rewards, and it leaves the delivery team's attention where it needs to be: migrating 30+ internal sources and the Teradata estate, not debugging cross-region conflict resolution.

3 · Cost case

Single production region means single-region infrastructure spend: one ADLS Gen2 footprint, one set of Event Hubs namespaces sized to 80K events/sec (not double), one ExpressRoute hub, and no steady-state cross-region egress or duplicated compute burn. The DR side carries only idle/minimal compute and scheduled replication traffic until an actual failover event. That shape leaves the most headroom of any topology to absorb the mandated 35% floor discount (3.3) on a 5-year fixed-price, no-escalator contract (3.1) without eroding margin to the point of risk — and it gives us room to negotiate rather than being cornered by our own cost base before Acme even asks a question. Under Net-90 annual-in-advance billing (3.2) with zero early-termination penalty exposure (3.5), a leaner cost base is also a leaner walk-away exposure if Acme exercises 30-day termination.

4 · Known weakness, addressed head-on

The gap: our contracted Enterprise tier is 99.95% monthly uptime, not the 99.99% Acme wants in 4.3, and 4.3 is unforgiving — any SLA miss of any duration triggers immediate termination and a full-month refund. I will not pretend this away. Two things follow from that, and we should get ahead of both in the proposal rather than let it surface in diligence.

First, this candidate's zone-redundant design (three AZs, zone-aware scheduling, automated failover) is a genuine uplift over a single-AZ 99.95% deployment in practical terms, even though our published contractual tier number doesn't move. We should propose it be reflected as an enhanced SLA rider with defined, automated failover time objectives and service-credit remedies tied to actual measured outage minutes — giving legal a negotiating position on 4.3 that ties termination to a real, monitored breach rather than any theoretical lapse.

Second, and more importantly: this candidate's cost discipline is the only thing that gives us room to negotiate 4.3 at all. If Acme will not move off literal 99.99%-with-termination, the fallback is the active-active add-on — and we can only afford to offer that add-on, inside the 35% floor discount and 5-year fixed price, because this topology didn't spend the budget on a second live region we didn't need. Bidding lean here is what preserves the option to bid rich there, if Acme insists.

Cand-03

Fabric-Fronted Hybrid: BTS-Synthetic Ingest/Storage with OneLake Serving

Eliminated
Topology

Split the platform along the ingest-versus-serve line and lean all the way into Acme being a Microsoft shop. BTS-Synthetic owns ingest and the lakehouse substrate: Event Hubs + the platform's streaming engine handling the 40k devices at 80k events/sec, the 80+ connectors doing batch ETL from the 30+ internal sources and the Teradata drawdown, and Delta tables written to ADLS Gen2 in West Europe (with an East US 2 stamp for non-EU data).

The serving tier is then handed to Microsoft Fabric: OneLake shortcuts point at our ADLS Gen2 Delta tables in place, and the 600 analysts consume them through Power BI Direct Lake semantic models rather than DirectQuery against our engine — no data copy, no separate BI extract layer. The 150 data engineers keep our low-code prep UI and notebooks for pipeline work; ML for predictive maintenance sits with our model registry/feature store, optionally surfaced to Azure ML. Availability is composed: our tier runs zone-redundant in West Europe, Microsoft's SLA covers the serving tier, and residency is enforced by pinning both the ADLS accounts and the Fabric capacity to EU regions.

Optimised for

RFP 2.3's non-negotiable Power BI requirement, taken to its maximum — Direct Lake is the fastest Power BI path that exists and directly attacks the Technical Fit "sub-second only on warmed caches up to 10TB" partial gap by moving the 600-user BI working set off our engine entirely; 2.3 open formats and portability (Delta in the customer's own ADLS, readable by anything, which also plays well to 4.4's demand that all work product vest in Acme); 3.x TCO, since we are not paying for a duplicated serving tier and part of the compute bill moves onto Acme's existing Azure/Fabric commitment; cultural fit with an Azure/Microsoft estate and the shortest path for 600 users who already live in Power BI.

Obvious weakness

It seats a named competitor inside our own architecture — Microsoft (Fabric) is listed in RFP section 6 as a rival bidder, and this design hands them the layer the customer actually touches every day, making us a replaceable ingest vendor by year two. Worse for the contract: it makes the SLA uncomputable. 4.3 demands 99.99% monthly with termination on any failure, but end-to-end availability here is the product of our tier and a Fabric capacity we neither operate nor control, and we would be carrying uncapped breach liability (4.1) across a boundary owned by a third party — which also collides head-on with 4.5, where every subprocessor needs Acme's prior written consent, withheld at their sole discretion. Split ownership also means split blame during incidents, and the delivery team has the least experience with this shape.

Position paper — Candidate 3 advocate

1 · Why this wins on the RFP's actual requirements

Read RFP 2.3 literally and this candidate is the only one built around its most concrete, non-negotiable line: "Native Power BI integration — this is non-negotiable, 600 users today." Every other candidate bolts a DirectQuery adapter onto our own engine and asks 600 analysts to query it. This candidate instead puts those 600 users on Power BI Direct Lake via OneLake shortcuts — zero-copy reads straight off our Delta tables in ADLS Gen2, no separate BI extract layer, no warmed-cache tier to provision or babysit. That directly retires the one technical gap Technical Fit flagged: "sub-second is only guaranteed on warmed caches up to 10TB" against a 280TB estate. Direct Lake doesn't need our cache warm — it reads the open Delta files live.

RFP 2.3 also asks for "open file formats (Parquet, Delta, Iceberg) for portability" and 4.4 requires all work product to "vest in Acme Corp upon creation." This candidate satisfies both simultaneously in a way no BI-extract architecture can: the data of record never leaves Delta format, never leaves Acme's own ADLS Gen2 accounts, and there is no proprietary serving-layer copy for anyone to argue about owning. Portability and IP vesting stop being contract clauses we have to defend and become properties of the deployment.

Section 1 of the RFP states plainly: "We are a Microsoft shop. Our primary cloud is Azure." That is not incidental — it is the single biggest fact about this buyer, and this candidate is the only one that takes it at face value rather than treating Fabric/Power BI as something to be walled off from. On evaluation criterion 1, functional fit (30%, the single largest weight), meeting 600 already-Microsoft-native users where they already live is a stronger fit story than asking them to adopt a new query surface.

None of this touches our core differentiation. RFP 2.1's real-time ingest from ~40,000 devices, 2.2's 80,000 events/sec peak (tested to 250K per Technical Fit), the 80+ connectors against 30+ internal sources, the Teradata drawdown through 2027, self-service prep for 150 data engineers, and the predictive-maintenance ML pipeline all stay exactly where they are today — on our platform, under our operational control. We are not diluting the product; we are routing the last mile of BI through the shortest path that exists for it.

2 · Delivery risk case

This is the smallest net-new build of any option on the table, because the integration surface is a shortcut, not a pipeline. OneLake shortcuts are metadata pointers — there is no new ETL job, no new replication topology, no new conflict-resolution layer to design, test, and operate. Compare that to what the RFP's own 15%-weighted "implementation timeline and risk" criterion actually has to worry about on this deal: a Teradata decommission running through 2027 that has to be sequenced against go-live. This candidate lets that migration proceed entirely on the ingest/storage side, untouched by whatever happens on the BI/serving side, which decouples two of the riskiest workstreams in the implementation plan instead of coupling them.

The scope our own engineering team owns end-to-end — streaming ingest, batch ETL, the lakehouse substrate, zone-redundant West Europe deployment with an East US 2 stamp for non-EU data — is the part of the platform we ship and operate every day. We are not asking the delivery team to stand up a novel multi-region active-active configuration or an unproven residency-pinning scheme under time pressure; we're asking them to do what they already do, and to hand off a well-documented, standards-based shortcut boundary to Fabric.

3 · Cost case — rough TCO shape

The largest cost lever in this bid is the one this candidate removes: a duplicated, always-on serving tier sized for 600 concurrent BI users across 280TB. There's no second fleet of warmed-cache query compute to provision, size, and keep running 24/7 on our side — that consumption moves onto Fabric capacity that Acme, as a stated existing Microsoft/Azure customer, most likely already budgets for or can absorb into an existing Microsoft agreement. Our infrastructure footprint stays to what it actually needs to be: zone-redundant ingest and storage in one primary region plus a lighter secondary stamp for non-EU data — not a second full production stamp.

That matters directly against RFP 3.1 (5-year pricing fixed at signature, no escalators) and 3.3 (35% discount floor off list). A leaner, non-duplicated infrastructure base gives more room to absorb a mandated 35% discount without the margin exposure that comes from carrying two full-scale serving stacks for five fixed years. It also plays straight into evaluation criterion 3, total cost of ownership (20% weight): the shape of this bid is "size compute once, for ingest and storage only," not "size compute twice, for ingest and for BI."

4 · Known weakness, addressed head-on

The honest version of this architecture's weakness is real and I'll state it plainly rather than let it surface in refutation: Microsoft Fabric is named as a competing bidder in RFP section 6, and this design places Fabric in the layer 600 users touch every day, which creates two problems — an SLA that spans an operational boundary we don't control, and a subprocessor question under RFP 4.5.

On 4.3's 99.99% clause: we do not propose to fake an end-to-end number we can't architecturally back — that's a losing bluff against the RFP's own "any SLA failure of any duration terminates the contract" language. Instead we scope our contractual uptime commitment precisely to the boundary we operate — the ingest/storage substrate, zone-redundant across West Europe with EU-pinned ADLS accounts — which is a number our architecture can actually underwrite, and we back it with Microsoft's own published Fabric capacity SLA passed through directly to Acme rather than absorbed and re-warranted by us. That correctly scopes 4.1's uncapped liability to the layer we actually control instead of accepting boundless exposure for a system we don't fully operate.

On 4.5 subprocessors: if the Fabric capacity is provisioned and owned directly in Acme's own Azure tenant — which, as a stated existing Microsoft/Azure shop, Acme is positioned to do — it is Acme's own resource, not one BTS-Synthetic "engages" on Acme's behalf, and the consent gate doesn't apply. We propose exactly that contract structure, not a workaround after the fact.

On the "replaceable ingest vendor" concern: it inverts. The hard, defensible IP in this deal — the 80+ connectors, 40K-device real-time ingest at 250K events/sec headroom, the predictive-maintenance model registry and feature store, the Teradata drawdown expertise — all stays ours, in open Delta files that vest in Acme per 4.4 regardless of what sits on top. Fabric isn't something we're conceding to a competitor; it's a layer Acme already owns and will use whether we integrate with it well or not.

Winner · Candidate 2

EU-Primary Zone-Redundant Lakehouse with Warm US East DR

Topology carried forward

A single active region — Azure West Europe — running the platform in its standard Enterprise tier, deployed zone-redundant across all three availability zones: ADLS Gen2 in ZRS, Event Hubs zone-redundant namespaces, platform compute pools spread across zones with zone-aware scheduling, and a zone-redundant metastore. All 280TB of primary data and all EU customer data live only in West Europe, making residency (2.3) a property of the deployment rather than a per-table policy to enforce and audit.

East US 2 is a warm standby: infrastructure-as-code pre-provisioned landing zone, curated Delta tables asynchronously replicated on a schedule (RPO in tens of minutes), minimal idle compute, promoted to primary only on a declared regional failure. IoT ingest terminates on a single global Event Hubs front door in West Europe with device-side buffering at the edge gateway; batch ETL and the Teradata decommissioning pipelines run in West Europe. Power BI DirectQuery from 600 users hits West Europe, with a curated import/aggregation layer plus a US-side read cache for Austin analysts. Networking is a single hub-and-spoke with Private Link, ExpressRoute into the EU hub, and the US spoke dormant but wired.

Why it holds

Best weighted commercial score in the field (5.8/10), and best on all three dimensions. Lowest sane cost for 2.2 scale; the simplest, most auditable form of EU residency against 4.2's no-notice audit right; the most room to absorb the 35% floor discount on a 5-year fixed price; and the standard shipped topology, so the fastest to stand up against the Teradata 2027 runway.

Weakness we ship with

It cannot honestly promise 99.99%. Zone redundancy raises real-world availability but the contracted Enterprise tier is 99.95%, and 4.3 makes any SLA miss of any duration a termination-plus-full-month-refund event. It also reads as non-compliant with "multi-region deployment (primary EU, secondary US East)" if Acme interprets that phrase as two serving regions rather than DR, and it puts 600 users' interactive BI on a single-region blast radius with a second-class US read experience.
